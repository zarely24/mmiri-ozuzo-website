Conversation first. Scissors where character is needed, a straight razor for the lines nothing else gets right, and talk that lasts as long as the cut.

The story

One barber, one 19th-century house.

Mmiri Ozuzo is the shop of Vasilis Monastirlis, one of the barbers who gave Athens back the idea of the barbershop as a place of craft and company. It sits in a neoclassical house on Kolokotroni Street with a history of its own.

I

The barber

Vasilis Monastirlis has been cutting hair for more than two decades. Before Mmiri Ozuzo he created “En Athinais 1928”; as he has put it himself, when he opened his first barbershop there were just two old barbershops in the whole Athens basin. He has served as artistic director of the Barber Awards at the Hairprof trade fair and has taught the history of the barbering craft. In 2023 Greek public broadcaster ERT devoted a video portrait to him titled “The man who brought barber shops to Greece”.

II

The house

The building at Kolokotroni 10 was built in the 19th century and was the home of the old Athenian Giatra–Belegrini family, who gave it its present form, most probably in the late 1870s. Local tradition holds that Theodoros Kolokotronis was hosted here; that account rests on references in his memoirs to a house in the area, not on documented historical record. The renovation that turned the house into a barbershop began in October 2015 and took three years: it kept the neoclassical shell and added a spare, industrial feel. According to the 2019 press feature, a four-metre ceiling painting, a copy after François Boucher, was also conserved.

III

The name

“Mmiri ozuzo” is a phrase from the Igbo language of Nigeria, which the founder has explained as meaning “storm”. The logo keeps it simple: Barber Salon. Hair, beards, shaves, and a room where the conversation lasts as long as the line.

Sources: iefimerida.gr feature (26 January 2019) and ERT video (6 October 2023). Details about the building come from the 2019 feature.
